# Audit: `garrett.yaml` against *The Elements of User Experience*

_22 Sep 2026. Read-only; nothing applied. Sources: S1 Pearson's 2nd-edition sample PDF (copyright page, full TOC with page numbers, both Introductions, all of Chapter 4, the index); S2 the original diagram, jjg.net/elements/pdf/elements.pdf, 30 March 2000; S3 1st-edition Chapter 2 in full; S4 1st-edition TOC; S5 Wikipedia; S6 jessejamesgarrett.com/about and /career-timeline; S7 jjg.net/about; S8 curl probes of every `author_links` URL; S9 Biblio/Amazon for ISBN 0321683684; S10 `docs/codebook futures/bristlenose-codebook-prompts-garrett-norman.md`; S11 `git log` on the YAML. Chapters 3, 5, 6, 7 are not legitimately online; claims resting on memory of them are marked INFERRED._

**The disease is confirmed.** S10 shows the Feb 2026 design had five tags, one per plane; the preamble is carried into the YAML verbatim. The YAML was born with 20 tags in its single commit (`8a528977`, 19 Feb 2026) and has never been revised. Of the 20 names, **8 are Garrett's own words, 7 are his concepts under our labels (two on the wrong plane), and 5 are not from the book.** The expansion also lost the model's second axis entirely.

## 2. Tag-by-tag

(a) Garrett's own term · (b) his concept, our label · (c) not from the book. Pages are 2nd edition (S1 TOC/index) unless noted.

**Strategy** — book: Product Objectives (Business Goals p. 37, Brand Identity p. 38, Success Metrics p. 39) | User Needs (User Segmentation p. 42, Usability and User Research p. 46, Creating Personas p. 49).

1. **user need** — (a) VERIFIED. "User Needs" ch. 3 p. 42; diagram "externally derived goals… identified through user research" (S2).
2. **business objective** — (b). His element is **Product Objectives** ("Site Objectives" in the 1st ed.), of which Business Goals is one of three sub-heads. Diagram: "business, creative, or other internally derived goals" — restricting to commercial intent narrows him.
3. **success metric** — (a) name VERIFIED (pp. 39–41), **definition contradicts him.** His success metrics are the organisation's quantified indicators ("success metrics make strategic goals quantifiable", ch. 4). Ours: "the participant's personal criteria for success" — a user need wearing the wrong name.
4. **value proposition** — (c) VERIFIED absent: not in either TOC or the index (no "value" entry). Marketing vocabulary (Lanning & Michaels 1988; Osterwalder). Already ships as Morville's Valuable facet. Meanwhile Garrett's third Strategy objective, **brand identity** (pp. 38–39), has no home on this plane.

**Scope** — book: Functional Specifications p. 68 | Content Requirements p. 71; Prioritizing Requirements p. 74.

5. **feature requirement** — (b). His terms are **functional specifications** and **requirements**; he uses "functional requirements" directly (ch. 4 ~p. 70); diagram "feature set". UXR has `feature request`; the `not_this` never distinguishes them.
6. **content requirement** — (a) VERIFIED (p. 71). Keep.
7. **priority** — (b). "Prioritizing Requirements" pp. 74–77: the team's prioritisation against objectives, needs and feasibility; ours is the participant's ranking. Acceptable lens shift; the name should say "requirement".
8. **scope creep** — (a). Garrett writes of "the dreaded 'scope creep'" (ch. 4 ~p. 65). Compatible; cite it.

**Structure** — book: Interaction Design (Conceptual Models p. 83, Error Handling p. 86) | Information Architecture (Structuring Content p. 89, Architectural Approaches p. 92, Organizing Principles p. 96, Language and Metadata p. 98).

9. **interaction design** — (a) VERIFIED (p. 81). But its `not_this` sends "a specific sequence of steps" to *task flow*, **contradicting the diagram**, where interaction design *is* "development of application flows to facilitate user tasks" (S2).
10. **information architecture** — (a) VERIFIED (p. 88; S3 "the arrangement of content elements within the information space"). Keep.
11. **navigation pattern** — (b), **on the wrong plane.** Navigation Design is a Skeleton element (ch. 6 p. 118; S2; S3 "the set of screen elements that allow the user to move through the information architecture"). The tag's definition splits one concept across two planes to justify the placement. Wayfinding (p. 127) is its sub-topic.
12. **task flow** — (c) VERIFIED absent: no TOC or index entry (index has "task analysis, 47" only). Generic IA-practice vocabulary (Dan Brown, *Communicating Design*, 2006). In Garrett it lives inside interaction design and under sequential structure (p. 95).

**Skeleton** — book: Convention and Metaphor p. 110; Interface Design p. 114 | Navigation Design p. 118; Information Design p. 124; Wayfinding p. 127; Wireframes p. 128.

13. **interface layout** — (b). His term is **Interface Design**: "arranging interface elements to enable users to interact with the functionality" (S3). "Page layout" (pp. 128–131) is a wireframe topic, not an element.
14. **wireframe issue** — (c) as a category. Wireframes (p. 128) is a deliverable a participant never sees. **The definition we gave it — content hierarchy, emphasis, reading order — is Garrett's Information Design** (p. 124; S2 "in the Tuftean sense: designing the presentation of information to facilitate understanding"). His term is absent by name and present in disguise.
15. **convention** — (a) VERIFIED (pp. 110–113). Keep. Its `not_this` sends visual styling norms to *brand alignment*; Garrett would send them to Surface external consistency (p. 143).
16. **component placement** — (b), pure padding. The Skeleton plane's own definition is "the placement of buttons, tabs, photos, and blocks of text… so that you… can find that shopping cart button" (S3 p. 22) — this tag's definition, under his term interface design. Tags 13 and 16 are one concept cut in two.

**Surface** — book (2nd ed.): Sensory Design — Making Sense of the Senses p. 135; Follow the Eye p. 137; Contrast and Uniformity p. 139; Internal and External Consistency p. 143; Color Palettes and Typography p. 145; Design Comps and Style Guides p. 148. (1st ed.: Visual Design.)

17. **visual design** — (a) VERIFIED for the 1st edition; the 2nd subsumes it under sensory design with Vision p. 136. Keep, cite both.
18. **sensory experience** — (b) at best. Indexed "sensory experience, 30" naming the *whole* Surface output in ch. 2, not a mood sub-category. Our definition ("feel, mood, atmosphere, the whole vibe") is Norman's visceral level / Morville `emotional appeal`.
19. **brand alignment** — (b). His term is **Brand Identity**, a Strategy objective delivered at Surface through external consistency (p. 143). Our definition drifts to credibility, which is Morville `trustworthiness` / `brand perception`.
20. **aesthetic reaction** — (c) VERIFIED absent from the index. INFERRED: ch. 7 judges surface decisions by whether they support the planes below, not by taste. Already ships twice: Morville `aesthetic quality`, Yablonski `Kurosu: beauty trust`.

## 3. Structure

- **Skeleton is the worst-mapped plane.** Garrett's three elements are interface design, navigation design, information design. Ours carries none by name: interface design split into `interface layout` + `component placement`; information design hiding as `wireframe issue`; navigation design pushed down to Structure. Only `convention` would be recognised from the TOC.
- **Structure holds a Skeleton element and an invented one**, while his indexed sub-topics — conceptual models, error handling, organizing principles, nomenclature — are unrepresented.
- **Surface has one recognisable tag of four**; the 2nd edition's sub-headings (contrast and uniformity; internal and external consistency; colour palettes and typography) are exactly what participants voice about a surface, and none is a tag.
- **Strategy lacks brand identity; Scope is the one plane that survives.**
- **The functionality/information duality is gone.**
- **The 4×5 symmetry is not the book's.** By his headings the planes are uneven: Strategy 2 elements, Scope 2, Structure 2, Skeleton 3, Surface 1. S10 proves the Feb 2026 design was 1×5; the 15 sub-tags were minted in the extraction commit.

## 4. Cross-references

**None.** Every `not_this` resolves to a tag in this file. No Norman tag name, old or new, appears, so nothing is stale from the 22 Sep rewording. Coincidental English overlaps with other codebooks' tag names (`goal`, `friction`, `worth it`, `unmet need`) are not references.
