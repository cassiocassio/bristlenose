## 2. Tag-by-tag

(a) his word/example (essay or his books) · (b) defensible elaboration of his facet paragraph · (c) not from Morville.

**Useful** — `relevance` (b, name collides: "relevance" is his own field's IR term, Findable vocabulary sitting in Useful); `problem-solution fit` (c, lean-startup); `utility` (c, Nielsen's term — Usability 101); `feature gap` (c, duplicates UXR `unmet need` / `feature request`). His paragraph is one thought and yields no sub-facets; four tags here are four ways of saying one word.

**Usable** — `ease of use` **(a)**, "Ease of use remains vital", the one R tag; `learnability` (c, Nielsen 1993); `efficiency` (c, Nielsen attribute — the word appears in the essay only in the **Desirable** paragraph as the thing to be tempered); `error tolerance` (c, Nielsen/ISO 9241-110; its `not_this` also mis-routes anticipatory worry to "a Credible concern about safety" — that is UXR `anxiety` or Nielsen `hesitation`). This group is Nielsen's five attributes minus two. Morville's paragraph explicitly *defers* usability to HCI methods ("We already had Jakob in last year") — importing Nielsen's decomposition here is the move he was declining to make.

**Desirable** — `emotional appeal` (b, "elements of emotional design", Norman 2004); `aesthetic quality` (b, closest word is "image"); `brand perception` **(a)**, "brand" and "identity" are his words; `engagement` (c, no source).

**Findable — the strongest group** — `navigation clarity` **(a)**, essay "design navigable web sites", *Ambient Findability* "supports navigation and retrieval"; `search effectiveness` (b, his books: "retrieval", "search systems", *Search Patterns* 2010); `content discoverability` (b, essay "locatable objects", AF "easy to discover or locate"); `labelling` (b, Rosenfeld & Morville's four IA systems). Every tag traceable; none needs to go.

**Accessible** — `inclusive design` (c name, Microsoft/Kat Holmes 2015–16; b content); `assistive compatibility` (b); `cognitive accessibility` (c and mis-scoped: the definition is cognitive load, Nielsen H8 / Yablonski `Sweller: cognitive overload`, not disability); `situational access` (c, Microsoft's persona spectrum; his sentence is disabilities only; duplicates UXR `device/channel`).

**Credible** — `trustworthiness` **(a)**, "trust and believe"; Stanford guideline 4; `content accuracy` (c via his citation: Stanford 1 and 8, which he points readers at); `transparency` (c via citation, definition drifted to "algorithms, business model, data collection" — 2010s privacy vocabulary, not 2002 Fogg; roots are Stanford 2, 5, 9); `social proof` (c, Cialdini; UXR `social influence` already has "the reviews said").

**Valuable — the group that contradicts the essay** — `worth it` (c, user-side); `competitive value` (c, positioning); `time well spent` (c, Tristan Harris 2013, fourteen years after the essay); `return intent` (c, NPS/retention). Morville's Valuable is explicit and three-part: **advance the mission** (non-profits), **contribute to the bottom line**, **improve customer satisfaction** (for-profits). The rewrite presumably judged the sponsor's bottom line uncodable from participant speech — but "I'd pay for this", "I'd switch", "I'd recommend it" *are* the participant-side evidence, and "it got me the benefit I came for" is mission evidence. The rewrite discarded the one facet that makes the honeycomb a client-facing framework.

**Tally:** verbatim-his (a): 4 (ease of use, brand perception, navigation clarity, trustworthiness). Defensible (b): 7. Not from Morville (c): 17 — sources are Nielsen (4), Fogg/Stanford (2), Microsoft inclusive design (2), lean startup, Cialdini, Harris, NPS, generic product-metrics vocabulary.
