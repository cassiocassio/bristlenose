The others are compressions: "Status visibility" (*Visibility of System Status*), "Real-world matching" (*Match Between the System and the Real World*), "Control and freedom" (*User Control and Freedom*), "Recognition over recall" (*Recognition Rather than Recall*), "Flexibility and efficiency" (*…of Use*), "Aesthetics and minimalism" (*Aesthetic and Minimalist Design*), "Errors and recovery" (*Help Users Recognize, Diagnose, and Recover from Errors*). ## 2. Tag-by-tag

(a) Nielsen's/NN/g's own term · (b) his concept, our label · (c) not from Nielsen. "Doc" = the design note records this as deliberate adaptation.

**H1 Visibility of system status** — `clear status` (b); `opaque status` (b, good discrimination silent ≠ unclear); `progress indication` (a, NN/g H1 "progress indicators for longer operations"); `mode confusion` **(c)** — no NN/g H1 text mentions modes; mode error is **Norman's** (1983; *DOET*), later Raskin's. Our own `norman.yaml` carries `mode error`, so this is a duplicate across the suite wearing Nielsen's badge.

**H2 Match between system and the real world** — `natural language` (b, "speak the users' language"); `jargon barrier` (b, "internal jargon"); `metaphor fit` (a-ish, H2 sub-article "Leverage Familiarity with Real-World Objects"); `logical ordering` (a, "natural and logical order", 1990 and 2020). Its `not_this` points at a retired Norman tag (§4).

**H3 User control and freedom** — `emergency exit` (a, verbatim); `trapped` (b, sub-article "prevent feeling trapped"); `undo available` (a, "Support Undo and Redo"); `destructive action` **(c) as placed** — H3 is about the *exit*; permanence of consequences is **H5** ("present users with a confirmation option before they commit", "Prevent high-cost errors first"). Its `not_this` already has to fend off H5 `hesitation` and H9, which is the tell. Also overlaps Norman `safeguard`.

**H4 Consistency and standards** — the most faithful group: `internal inconsistency` (a), `platform convention` (a, "external consistency", Jakob's Law), `terminology inconsistency` (a). Narrow-scope rule (explicit comparison language) is Doc-recorded adaptation.

**H5 Error prevention** — `hesitation` (c, **Doc-recorded** adaptation); `near miss` (c, Doc-recorded; note NN/g's own H5 vocabulary is *slips vs mistakes*, which we correctly gave to Norman); `guardrail` (b, "helpful constraints and good defaults"; its `not_this` names a retired Norman tag); `risky default` (b, negative pole of "Choose Good Defaults", which NN/g files under **H5** — the positive pole we filed under H6, next).

**H6 Recognition rather than recall** — `visible options` (a); `memory burden` (a); `helpful default` **(b) but misfiled by NN/g's own map**: defaults are H5; what NN/g puts under H6 is "History and Recently Visited Content", suggestions, "easily retrievable". The definition ("pre-fills, suggests, or remembers") already describes the H6 concept — only the *name* is wrong. Rename, don't move. `context loss` **(c)** — not in the H6 definition, tips or sub-article; "losing the thread" is wayfinding (Morville `navigation clarity`) or a UXR behaviour. Closest thing to padding in the file.

**H7 Flexibility and efficiency of use** — `shortcut desire` (a); `repetitive task` (a, "Accelerators Improve Repetitious Usage"); `customisation` (a, "Allow for customization"; personalization left out, Doc-recorded).

**H8 Aesthetic and minimalist design** — `information overload` (b); `focused design` (b); `competing elements` (b, "competes with the relevant units of information"). Its `not_this` says "discoverability (Norman)" — a Norman *group*, not a tag.

**H9 Help users recognise, diagnose, and recover from errors** — `clear error message` (b); `cryptic error` (a-ish, "(no error codes)" verbatim); `blame language` (a, NN/g *Error Message Guidelines* names *invalid*, *illegal*); `recovery guidance` (a, "constructively suggest a solution").

**H10 Help and documentation** — `self-service help` (b, "Reactive Help"); `help absent` (b); `contextual guidance` (a, "present the documentation in context").

**Contradictions with Nielsen:** none of substance. One soft one: H5's `hesitation`/`near miss` observe the *participant*, where Nielsen's H5 says prevention is the *designer's* job — the doc acknowledges exactly this and adopts the symptom deliberately. Recorded, so not a defect.

**Tally:** (a) 16 · (b) 15 · (c) 5 — of which 2 are Doc-recorded adaptations (`hesitation`, `near miss`) and 3 are not (`mode confusion`, `destructive action` as placed, `context loss`). Nothing rises to an invented tag with fabricated provenance; the file never claims a (c) tag is his.

## 3. Structure

- All 10 in Nielsen's order. VERIFIED.
- Group names: 3/10 verbatim; 7 compressed, uncredited as a decision.
- Subtitles are ours, in question form; each paraphrases fairly.
- What an NN/g reader would not recognise: `mode confusion`, `context loss`, `destructive action` under Control and freedom, `helpful default` under Recognition.
- Group sizes 4/4/4/3/4/4/3/3/4/3 = 36. The three 3-tag groups (H4, H7, H10) are the ones the doc flags as sparse — the model did **not** pad them, the opposite of the Norman finding. The padding, such as it is, went into the 4-tag groups.

**Bottom line.** Far better shape than Norman v1: 31 of 36 tags trace to Nielsen's or NN/g's own text, the description is accurate, the citations check out, and the deliberate adaptations are written down. The real defects: one bio fact, seven compressed group names nobody recorded compressing, two tags that aren't his, one misfiled, one misnamed, and five `not_this` pointers left dangling by the Norman rewording — the last being the only thing that will actively mislead AutoCode today, since a pointer to a tag that no longer exists is a discrimination hint the model can't follow.
