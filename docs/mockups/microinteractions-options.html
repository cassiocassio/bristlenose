Should the report move more — and where?

Bounces and slides on opens and closes, a flash when a tag is accepted, a staggered fly-up when several quotes are hidden at once. Three sizes of answer, each demonstrated live, each shown under Reduce Motion, with what it costs and what it rules out.

The question, as found at HEAD

The row dates from March. Since then most of its ask has shipped, and one part of it was deliberately taken out:

  • Opens and closes. Both sidebars slide at 75 ms (organisms/sidebar.css, tuned through three rounds, docs/design-motion.md §3). Every dialog drops in as the macOS sheet and rises out (atoms/modal.css, 24 Oct 2026, 0d482d246). The four toolbar dropdowns (tag filter, view switcher, hidden-quotes counter, tag suggest) are still display:none — instant (design-motion R5, R6, R10, open since March).
  • Flashes of acceptance. Shipped: badge-accept-flash on the quote card’s new badge and on the sidebar tag row (0.4 s brightness). Shipped: the arrival flash on anything a link lands on, tokenised (atoms/arrival.css, 17 Oct). Shipped: a 240 ms dim as a lens hands over to fresh data (bn-handover, 23 Oct). Q8 (confirm-flash-options.html) is about making the first of these reusable.
  • Staggered fly-up for bulk hide. A single hide flies a ghost of the card up to the “N hidden quotes” counter and collapses the card in 300 ms (QuoteGroup.tsx). Bulk hide has no stagger and no ghost: on 22 Sep 2026 it became one store call, one timer and one PATCH for the whole selection, because the per-card version wrote n times and missed cards in other groups (QuotesContext.tsx writeHide, frontend gotchas “A per-item store call inside a bulk gesture is still n writes”). Unhide-all does stagger, at 150 ms per card, flying down from the counter. So the asymmetry is the remainder of this row.
  • Reduce Motion. Per-block guards, with a set-equality test shape (test_every_transitioned_element_is_zeroed_under_reduced_motion for Focus Mode). The five badge keyframes are unguarded. docs/design-motion.md §5.3 recommends one global rule in tokens.css that exempts the arrival flash; not landed. The arrival flash is unguarded by decision: a colour fade moves nothing. The Mac side already gates on accessibilityReduceMotion (shimmer, Welcome).
  • The 150 ms figure contradicts the house formula. The row says 150 ms per card; design-motion §6 says stagger = min(50 ms, 300 ms / N), total stagger capped at 300 ms. Frame 1 shows both at 4 and at 20 cards.

Reliability-phase note: “beta means (almost) no new features” (19 Oct 2026). Every option below is weighed against that, not only against the motion brief.

Commentary

What each column adds. Today: the four cards collapse together; the counter goes 2 → 6 with no cue, so nothing says where they went. A: the cards leave in reading order, each 50 ms after the last, and the counter flashes once (.bn-confirm-flash, Q8) as its number changes: order and destination are said, in 450 ms. C: 150 ms between cards, each with its own fly-up ghost to the counter, as the single hide does: the richest picture, and for four cards still under a second.

The result must never wait for the motion. Today the store holds the quotes in a hiding set and commits them (one write, one Undo entry) when its single 300 ms timer fires. A stagger either delays that commit until the last card has gone, or commits early and lets React drop the cards mid-flight. A carries the delay on the card (--bn-hide-delay set per card in CSS) and extends the one timer by the capped total, so the commit lands by 600 ms at most, however many cards. C with 150 ms uncapped has no such bound: see the chart.

Under Reduce Motion (toggle above): the collapse and ghosts are instant in every column; A and C keep the counter’s flash, because it is colour and carries the information the motion carried. Today gives Reduce Motion users nothing at all: this is the one place an informational cue is currently absent for them.

Ghosts cost n clones. The single-hide ghost is a cloneNode(true) appended to body, animated with transition: all, removed on a timer. Twenty of them is twenty detached subtrees painting over the report while the real cards (`content-visibility: auto` below the fold) animate nothing visible. WebKit is the Mac app’s engine; max-height and margin transitions run on the main thread there.

Commentary

Direct manipulation has a 0.1 s budget for the acknowledgement (the card starts to go) and the house already accepts 300 ms for the commit. A stays inside the 300 ms + 300 ms envelope at any N. C at 150 ms per card reaches 3.2 s for twenty quotes and 7.7 s for fifty, during which the researcher can neither undo nor see the new count; and a second h on a card still in flight is already filtered out by the store, so it would simply be swallowed. What this costs in React, not CSS. Every dropdown today is {open && <div>}: on close the node is gone before any exit can draw. The options

A Restraint — motion only where it carries information

siblings: single-hide ghost (QuoteGroup.tsx) · arrival (atoms/arrival.css) · handover (toolbar.css)
What changes
  • Bulk hide gets the capped stagger (min(50 ms, 300 ms/N)) as a per-card transition-delay; the store’s one timer grows by the total stagger (≤ 300 ms). The hidden-quotes counter flashes .bn-confirm-flash when its count changes (hide, unhide, bulk).
  • The global Reduce Motion rule from design-motion §5.3 lands in tokens.css, exempting the arrival and confirm cues; badge keyframes stop being unguarded.
  • Dropdowns, star, view switch: unchanged. Design-motion’s open rows R5/R6/R10/K1/S1 are marked “not pursued in beta”.

Consequences
  • S build — CSS: hidden-quotes.css, tokens.css, badge.css; TS: QuotesContext.tsx (timer length), QuoteGroup.tsx (set --bn-hide-delay on hiding cards). Regenerate WelcomeTimelineCSS.swift (any theme edit is a desktop edit).
  • Tests: extend the Focus-mode set-equality shape to the global rule; one vitest on the timer bound; a Playwright check under emulateMedia({reducedMotion}). jsdom cannot see transitions, so the stagger itself is pinned on the style attribute, not on behaviour.
  • Export: the CSS travels, but hide and tag controls are hidden in export mode, so none of this fires there. WKWebView: delays on the leaf card, not a custom property on the group (a property on the subtree root restyles every descendant in WebKit).
  • Rules out nothing: B and C stack on top later.
  • Risk: the global Reduce Motion rule changes ~280 declarations at once for Reduce Motion users: a one-line change with a wide blast; the exemption list must be tested, or it silently ends the arrival flash.

B A vocabulary of three tokens, applied everywhere

siblings: --bn-modal-enter/-exit (tokens.css § Dialogs) · --bn-arrival-* · --bn-transition-fast/normal/slow
What changes
  • Three tokens — --bn-motion-enter 120 ms ease-out, --bn-motion-exit 90 ms ease-in, --bn-motion-confirm 400 ms ease-out — and three atoms .bn-enter, .bn-exit, .bn-confirm-flash.
  • The four dropdowns adopt enter/exit (Frame 2, middle). Badge appear/remove (150 ms today) move onto the tokens. The accept flash becomes the confirm atom (Q8 option C).
  • Everything A does.
Consequences
  • M build — the CSS is a morning; the React is the cost: four components keep a closing state so the exit can draw, each with Escape, outside-click and focus-return paths to keep right. Layers: theme atoms + molecules, four components, their tests, the e2e config (reducedMotion: 'reduce' for determinism).
  • Three tokens cannot hold the shipped durations: sidebar 75 ms, sheet 230/240 ms, arrival 5 s, handover 240 ms, badge 150 ms were each tuned separately. Either they stay as named exceptions (a vocabulary of three plus five) or they are folded and the tuning is lost. The sidebar took three rounds to reach 75 ms.
  • Perceived latency: an exit that must finish before a new dropdown can open is a 90 ms wait on a toolbar the researcher drives fast. Must be interruptible (reverse mid-flight, as CSS transitions do).
  • Rules out: the {open && …} simplicity in every dropdown, for good.
  • Feature-shaped work in a reliability phase.

Recommendation: A, with Q8’s confirm atom as its one new piece

Most of this row has shipped since it was written, and the remaining gap is an asymmetry (unhide staggers, hide does not) plus an accessibility hole (Reduce Motion users get no cue when quotes leave). A closes both with the house stagger formula, keeps the commit inside the envelope the store already promises, and gives Reduce Motion users a colour cue where today they get none. It touches two TS files and three CSS files, and it rules nothing out: B’s tokens and C’s dropdown slides can land on top when a feature phase opens. B and C are feature work: the sidebar’s history says each new motion takes rounds to tune, and the beta line says not now.

Not 150 ms per card. The number came from the frozen vanilla unhide, where cards arrive and the count is already right. On a leave, it holds the researcher’s Undo hostage to N. If the owner wants a slower cadence than 50 ms, the honest form is a higher cap (say 450 ms total), not a per-card figure.

Owner calls the mockup could not settle: (1) stagger cadence: the formula’s 50 ms cap, or a named total; (2) under Reduce Motion, the counter flash as the substitute for the fly-up, or nothing; (3) land the global Reduce Motion rule now (wide blast, one line) or keep per-block guards and only fix the badge block.
