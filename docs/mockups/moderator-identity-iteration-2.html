CommentaryWhy the frame looks like that, the rows behind it, and what Phase 2 still has to decide. Deliberately not the product.

The cast. Ward handover app — round 2, six sessions. Three came in from Teams with the platform's own speaker labels (Martin B Storey twice, Kerri Ng once, Jane Smith as an observer); three are recordings dropped in by hand, where nothing names the moderator. The researcher is Martin Storey, whose Mac account name is Martin Storey — not quite the spelling Teams uses, and nothing in the product will try to reconcile the two for him.

Read each frame as a row. A session's moderator slot is one of null · proposed · confirmed. The badge draws the state and nothing else: no origin chip, no "heard in", no sentence. Where a name came from lives in the evidence column and in the picker's behaviour, not on the page.

Three states, one atom. Confirmed is the split badge as it ships today. Proposed is the same badge wearing .badge-proposed: the 3 s pulse, the dashed edge, and the ✗ | ✓ pill on hover and on keyboard focus — exactly what an AutoCode-proposed tag looks like on a quote card, so the researcher has already learned it. Null is a code lozenge reading m? with no name half; the shipped :last-child rule gives it the full radius without a new selector.

The one new rule. .badge-proposed adds a dashed border, which on a single-box .badge is the whole treatment. The split badge is two boxes that already carry a solid border each, so the class on the wrapper draws a dashed ring around a solid pair. Three lines move the dash onto the halves (see the second <style> block). That is the entire CSS cost of this iteration.

✓ and ✗ mean what they mean on a tag. Accept confirms the slot with the proposed identity. Deny rejects the proposal: the slot goes back to null and the row reads m?; the identity that was minted from the label stays minted (numbers are never reused), it simply covers one session fewer. Titles are the shipped buttons.accept / buttons.deny.

Code-only mode is free. The shipped data-person-display="code" switch (toolbar above) hides every name half; m? and m1 survive it unchanged, which is the point of keeping the gap in the code rather than in a sentence.

The first thing real pixels found. The pill is positioned to hang past the badge's right edge by 1 rem (atoms/badge.css: ✗ sits where a tag's delete × sits, ✓ overhangs). On a quote card that overhang is whitespace. In the grid the name starts 0.35 rem to the right of the lozenge, so the ✓ compartment lands on the first letter of Martin. Drawn as-is on purpose: it is the true consequence of composing two shipped idioms, and the storyboard's hand-drawn frames could not have shown it. Phase 2 has two cheap fixes — hang the pill to the left inside .bn-session-speaker-entry (the thumbnail cell's padding is empty ground), or open the entry's gap to 1.4 rem while the pill is visible. One rule either way; the recommendation is the first, so the name never moves.

Two controls, two families. The pill is on the code lozenge and acts on the slot (accept or reject this proposal). The pencil is on the name and acts on the identity (retype "Martin B Storey" as "Martin Storey", everywhere it appears). §B2's rule — rename and reassign never share a control — falls out of the grid's existing decomposition without a new element.

Keyboard. The pill's compartments are real buttons with the house inset focus ring; a / d can be bound as on quote cards. The lozenge is focusable (tabindex="0"), so :focus-within reveals the pill without a mouse.
