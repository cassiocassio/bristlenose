  • The trigger decides whether this is useful or a nag. Today the out-of-credit pill lights from a sticky verdict (Settings validation or a failed run) and stays until a top-up and a successful run. For the hand-tagger that is a pill present for months over work that never needed the provider. A′ is the alternative: the pill appears when a call was refused, not when a verdict is merely known.
  • The glyph is the existing MessageKind, not a new one. Rejected key is error (xmark.circle.fill, red; .invalid is red everywhere else); credit and unreachable are warning (exclamationmark.triangle.fill, amber). The words say which. This replaces the amber dot the shipped out-of-credit pill uses, a documented decision to reverse.
  • The diagnosis decides who the sentence is about. Offline: no pill. Commentary · how it behaves, and the calls that are yours
  • No ✕ convention for a toolbar pill in the HIG. The closest are system notification banners and tip popovers, which both give people a dismiss and say to “make it easy to dismiss”. A trailing ✕ is a reasonable invention; it is not a precedent.
  • Decided (owner, 2 Nov 2026): ✕ is the dismiss and hides the notice entirely; no Hide button in the popover; no menu-bar twin. The ✕ is a well-known affordance (Safari’s tab close button is the comparison), and a micro-interaction on a small flag belongs on the surface, not in the menu bar, which is for discovering general commands. Both reviewers asked for a menu twin so a hidden toolbar or the » overflow can’t lose the condition. That cost is accepted: the Settings dot still says it, and a new refused call or a changed condition brings the pill back. The VoiceOver “Hide” action on the main button stays, so VoiceOver users don’t need to find the ✕. The collapsed glyph is bare, with no capsule, and has no ✕ of its own yet.
  • Decided (owner, 2 Nov 2026): the ✕ shows on any mouseover into the pill, like Safari’s tabs, and on keyboard focus; its space is reserved. Reserving it means the pill never resizes under the pointer, which in the centred .status zone would also move the glyph and its neighbours. Two layouts are in the native prototype for comparison: slot on the right only (lopsided at rest) and slot balanced on both sides (about 20 pt wider). Two crosses in the error state are accepted (“just unfortunate”: there is no stronger red failure mark and no better-understood close mark). With hover-reveal they are on screen together only while hovering or focused.
  • ✕ has to be a separate hit target. StatusPill is one Button that toggles the popover, so a nested ✕ needs its own target beside it. Right-click “Dismiss” isn’t available on a toolbar item (macOS swallows it for Customize Toolbar; already a gotcha in desktop/CLAUDE.md). So the accessible dismiss is the Hide button in the popover (fourth frame), with ✕ as the pointer shortcut. A hover-only ✕ would be unreachable by keyboard and VoiceOver.
  • Dismissed doesn’t mean forgotten. ✕ removes the pill; the Settings dot still says “Out of credit”, and the next refused call re-lights it.
  • When does it come back loud? The risk in your own rule: a month of transcript edits on empty credit means a month of refused calls, and if each one re-lights the full pill, that is the nag again. Suggested rule: a refusal re-expands the pill only when the condition changed (credit → key rejected) or the call was an explicit AI action such as Analyse or Autocode. An incidental refusal on an already-acknowledged condition gets its in-place note and nothing in the title bar.
  • Clearing. A successful call, a re-validated key, or a top-up removes it at any stage. App-wide state, so hiding it in one window hides it in all.
  • Not checked. The exact SF Symbols (the glyphs here are drawn to suggest them), the ✕’s hit-target size, and how a glyph-only item behaves in macOS 26 toolbar overflow.

Reading it. The pill is for 5, 6, 7, 11 and 12, plus 8 if you want a persistent outage shown. Rows 5–7 are three ways to detect one condition and say the same words, so they differ only in the details the log keeps. Rows 1–4, 9, 10 and 13 are about the person’s own situation, a transient limit or an unknown, so they are said where it happened and never become a pill. A VPN that dropped can’t be told from rows 2 or 5, so it lands in 13 and says nothing it can’t support.

Remedies assume nothing about who the person is. No line says “ask your administrator”: this could be a café, a plane or a hotel, and for them a different network or a different provider is the only lever. Where another provider is known to work, the pull-down (“Switch to…”) is the action under line 2. For someone who does have an administrator, the popover can still offer a quiet Copy details, as the run-failure popover already does.

Not yet measured, and how to get it. Rows 1, 2 and 3 need the real states: Wi-Fi off, a router with the WAN unplugged, a captive-portal network. Rows 4–7 can be induced locally: a wrong clock (revert it after), a blocking rule in a firewall tool or a null address in the hosts file for the provider, and a local proxy that re-signs TLS or returns a 403 page. Rows 8 and 9 need a stub that answers 529 and 429 with the provider’s JSON. For each, record the URLError code, the HTTP status and headers, the control-probe result and the path state. I can write that as one script.

  • The last rung was mislabelled and is now two. “Claude answering” read as “is Claude alive?”, so a ⚠ on it said Claude was silent, when in the credit and key cases Claude replied and refused. “Claude responding” fails only when Claude itself is failing (an outage); “Your account” fails when Claude replied and said no (key, credit, rate limit).   • The sign-in rung exists only when a sign-in page was actually seen. At home or at work there is no such page, so nobody’s ladder has that row; it is inserted for the café case (five bars, nothing loads, the sign-in screen hidden somewhere). macOS’s own assistant handles the first sign-in; this is for when it didn’t appear or the portal wants you again. What counts as “seen”: a plain-HTTP request to Apple’s check page that returns anything but “Success” (a login page or a redirect). Over HTTPS a portal can’t be told from a wrong clock or an inspecting proxy (all one TLS error), so it is no evidence, and with no evidence the row stays out. That means a two-step probe: HTTPS first, then HTTP only if the first fails with a TLS error, so cleartext traffic is rare. It needs a narrow transport-rules exception for Apple’s host in the app’s Info.plist (the project already merges a partial plist for the OAuth keys). Untested on a real portal.
  • It answers “whose side is it on?” without asking anyone. Everything up to “Internet” is the person’s situation, “Claude reachable” is the network between them and the provider, “Claude responding” is whether the provider itself is working, and “Your account” is whether it will take this request. That’s the distinction you’re asking for; the two lines name the rung, the ladder shows the evidence for it.
  • Below a failure is “not checked”, never “fine”. If the router and internet fail, claiming Claude is reachable would be a guess. This is what the skipped kind is for.
  • Default closed. The popover opens on the two lines. The ladder sits behind a disclosure (“Connection details”, proposed wording), so the quick case stays quick and nobody is asked to read a diagnostic they didn’t want. Popovers should hold “a small amount of information”.
  • It can live where checks already live. Diagnostics ▸ Check Health is a native list of pass/fail checks, and a “Connection” section there would be the same ladder run on demand. I haven’t read that window’s code for network checks.
  • It costs requests the pill doesn’t. The pill needs a diagnosis only after a refused call. The ladder adds the control probe, a DNS check and the keyless provider request, run once at that moment, never on a timer.
