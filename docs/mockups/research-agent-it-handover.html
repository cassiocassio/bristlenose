Research agent: IT's experience

Companion to docs/design-research-agent.md. A quick sketch (Tier 1): hand-rolled styles, and every word is proposed copy nobody has reviewed. It draws the IT admin's jobs as a flow, one frame per scenario; the ids (I1, P7…) match the PRD's §7. How each step is built is parked (PRD Appendix A).

The cast is invented: Sam Okafor, a freelance researcher; Priya Shah, an in-house researcher; Halden, the client; Product X, its product.

Artefact: what the person would see Commentary: why, and which scenario

Bristlenose drafts this email at publish time, because "what do I even ask IT for?" is where this dies (P2). Sam doesn't need to know the technical words; the email uses words IT knows, and the ask is the PRD's one sentence (principle 6).

It names who owns it after Sam leaves, because a tool nobody owns is IT's biggest worry (§3.2).

2. One page to decide on

What we know about Product X · for ITI1 · I2

Research prepared by Sam Okafor on 14 Mar 2027

Sam Okafor asks you for somewhere to run this that only people signed in with your accounts can reach.

Set it up Security details Ask Sam a question

What it is

Three user-research studies about Product X that your colleagues can ask questions of, from Teams, Slack, Figma Make, Claude or ChatGPT. It answers with quotes from the research and can't change anything.

What's in it

  • 3 studies: onboarding, pricing, admin console
  • 412 quotes chosen by the researcher, from 36 participants
  • Participants appear as codes (p1, p2…), never by name
  • No video, no audio, no full transcripts
  • Stops answering on 31 Mar 2028

Who's involved

  • It runs on your infrastructure, behind your sign-in
  • You choose who can ask, from your existing groups
  • No one outside Halden holds the data, including the makers of Bristlenose

How to remove it

One action removes it and everything in it. No copy is kept anywhere else.

Commentary

I1 and I2: the page leads with the ask, then answers what a reviewer decides on, in that order. "How to remove it" is on the first screen on purpose: it's the question that gets things approved.

"Security details" holds the checklist (signature, software list, network, logs) for whoever reviews it. That's real content, but implementation detail, so it's parked in the PRD's Appendix A and not drawn here.

Commentary

I3: two decisions IT is good at, where it runs and who gets in, and nothing else. I4: offering it to everyone in a role is adding the role's group under "Who can ask"; offering it to one team is adding that team. Membership stays where IT already manages it, Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace, so there's no second user list.

Asking and adding research are separate on purpose: a guest freelancer can be allowed to publish until a date without being able to see anyone else's collections.

Whether "Set Up" can really be one guided step on each platform is an implementation question (Appendix A). The experience goal is that IT never needs a developer.

Commentary

The banner shows the moment before 09:40, the activity shows the day as it went; both are on one frame to save space.

P7 and I7: Sam returns five months later. The only thing IT does is renew one permission: no re-install, no new name for the team. Sam's earlier three studies stay as they were, and Sam doesn't need them back.

P6 and I8: Priya works on her own Mac and publishes a minute before Sam. Both land; neither touches the other's studies. There is nothing for IT to resolve.

The banner is the only thing that asks IT to act, and it names who, what, since when, and the one action.
