Research agent: the researcher publishing

Companion to docs/design-research-agent.md. A quick sketch (Tier 1): hand-rolled styles that only approximate the Mac app, and every word is proposed copy nobody has reviewed. It draws the publisher's jobs (PRD §3.1) as a flow, one frame per scenario; the ids (P1…P10) match the PRD's §7.1.

The cast is invented: Sam Okafor, a freelance researcher; Priya Shah, an in-house researcher; Halden, the client; Product X, its product.

The future Report lens, which the PRD makes a prerequisite (§5). It is the researcher's argument: the signals they stand behind, the quotes they starred, the clips worth watching. Publish lives here and nowhere else, so what gets published is always what the researcher chose (principle 4).

P10, the zero-sign-in route: Bristlenose saves the pack into the cloud folder already on the Mac, then walks the researcher through the last steps, which only they can do in the builder. No sign-in screen, no "needs admin approval".

"Not on this Mac" is stated as a fact, not hidden: Google Drive still works through "Another folder…" or a later sign-in route (PRD §9 Q7). The tabs show the same four steps in each builder's own words.

Step 3's instructions carry the rules the research agent enforces itself: answer with quotes, say when the research doesn't cover something, don't count beyond the numbers given (asker mockup, frame 8).

Commentary

P8: the researcher with a Netlify-style experiments platform. There's no "publish anyway": quotes on the open internet aren't a trade-off a researcher can make on participants' behalf. The title states the consequence in plain words; the body says what was checked and the one way forward. A single OK, because there's nothing to choose.

How Bristlenose tells that an address "answered without asking who you are" is implementation, parked (PRD Appendix A). The experience is what's drawn.
