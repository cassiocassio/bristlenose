Leader styles over a busy screengrab

The same synthetic board drawn eight ways. The shop page, quotes and participants are invented. Everything else uses the shipped tokens, the shipped quote card at the label size, the sequence badge and the highlight box. Card placement follows docs/design-screens.md S5: cards sit beside the screengrab in line order, and every card is joined by a leader to its line.

A

Today

baseline
Commentary
  • Over the gutter it is the Discussion wire, and it works. Over the page, the 1px accent is 1.0–1.6:1 against the blue button, the link blue and the bag photo, so it reads as part of the page or disappears.
  • The target box goes first: its 1.5px accent border around Add to bag is 1.4:1 against the button. The leader then points at nothing visible.

B

Cased

rank 1
Commentary
  • A stroke in --bn-colour-bg under the core. Over the gutter it matches the board and cannot be seen, so off the page this is exactly A and exactly the Discussion wire. Over the page it gives the core a guaranteed neighbour: 4.0:1 default light, 5.2:1 default dark, 6.7:1 Edo light, 6.2:1 Edo dark.
  • The same 1px casing goes round the target box (box-shadow) and the end dot (paint-order: stroke), so the target survives the blue button too.
  • Precedents: cartographic casing (Esri), Richards's "thin white line beside" the leader in technical illustration, and Skitch's white outline, here without Skitch's weight.
  • The selected leader goes to 1.5px, as the Discussion's hot wire does.
  • Build: leaderPath unchanged; one extra path per leader; about five CSS lines.
  • Revised in B′: the casing in the board's colour turns into heavy dark tracks in dark mode, and into white tracks over a dark-mode site. The casing should take the screengrab's paper colour instead. See B′.
C

Cased + lifted

variant of B
Commentary
  • B plus the cards' own shadow on the leader. It says the leader belongs to the floating layer, not the page: in S5 terms, "only what floats has a shadow".
  • It reads more clearly over the photo, and heavier everywhere, including over clear space, where the Discussion look is lost. Discussion went from 1.3/2px to 1px because heavier strokes "read chunky at real size".
  • Worth an A/B against B on a real session; not the first move.
D

Gutter-run (cased)

rank 3
Commentary
  • Where a card sits far from its line, the long travel happens in clear space and only a short slant crosses the page. That answers the owner's "long leaders run across the screenshot".
  • The final segment stays slanted on purpose: a web page is full of horizontal and vertical rules, and an orthogonal segment over it reads as an underline or a divider (the "floorplan" finding in Bekos et al.).
  • Cost: several leaders running down one 32px gutter need a track each, or they run alongside one another. Close parallel leaders are the opo failure mode, measured worst by Barth et al. Measure how many leaders are long on real sessions before building it.
E

Edge port + badge on the target

not as default
Commentary
  • At rest no line crosses the page: the leader stops at the edge with a cased chevron, level with its line, and the target carries the card's own p1 ① badge. Selecting the card draws the rest of the line.
  • This gives the cleanest screengrab, but the join becomes a lookup: scan along the row to the badge. Figma's comment pins show that marks needing a second step get read less. It also breaks S5's "a linked quote has a visible leader".
  • The badges are themselves marks on the page. Two quotes about one line need two badges, and here they sit over the price above the button. So the screengrab is cleaner only in lines, not in marks.
  • Keep it as the mode for a dense screen (12+ links), not the default. Rows already cover the narrow case.
F

Spotlight on selection (with B)

rank 2
Commentary
  • Set State to One selected, or click a card. The screengrab outside the lit line takes a 45% scrim of the board's background. This is the cartographer's area of disinterest: quieten the context rather than shout the subject.
  • Only while a card is selected (a press, focus, J/K), never at rest and never on hover. The artefact is unchanged until the researcher asks about one line, which keeps the owner's rule about respecting the screengrab.
  • Build: one box-shadow on .screens-hl.on under .focus, clipped to the slide. box-shadow is already allowed by the selected-state gate.
G

Ink (cased)

second best of the colours
Commentary
  • A neutral line separates itself from a blue page by hue and luminance (17:1 against its casing). It is the drafting tradition exactly.
  • It reads heavier than the accent, and it drops the meaning BN's accent carries (linked, interactive). Without the casing it would fail on dark regions (1.7:1 on the bag's shadow side).
H

Difference blend

rejected
Commentary
  • "Adapts to any background" until the background is a mid-tone: over the grey tile or the photo's shadow side the line becomes a mid-tone too, and over blue it turns orange. Its colour can't be themed, and its 1.4.11 contrast can't be stated.
  • Shown so the failure is visible, not as a candidate.
B′

Casing that follows the screengrab

rank 1: R3, chosen
Variant
Commentary
  • Why B failed in dark. B's casing took the board's background. In dark mode that is near-black, drawn over a screengrab that is almost always white, so every leader gained two dark tracks: heavy, and visibly not part of either surface. The mirror case is a light board over a dark-mode site, where the casing becomes white tracks on a dark page. Pick R0 to see both.
  • R4, the same casing at half strength and half width, only softens the tracks. It also loses the guarantee: half a casing is not an adjacent colour you can measure.
  • R1, casing in the screengrab's own paper colour, clipped to the screengrab. This is the print cartographer's mask rather than a halo. On paper the casing is the paper and cannot be seen; it appears only where the line crosses ink (the button, the photo, text). In the gutter there is no casing at all. So the line looks like a bare Discussion wire everywhere it can, in all four combinations.
  • R1 is enough for the default palette, not for Edo. The core keeps the board's accent. Edo light #0f5c9e on a dark site's paper is 2.7:1, and Edo dark #4d9fe0 on a white page is 2.9:1. Switch the palette to Edo to see it.
  • R2 adds one rule: over the screengrab the line takes the accent of the screengrab's scheme. A dark site gets the dark-mode accent, a light site the light-mode one, whatever the board is doing. Against the paper: default 4.0:1 (light) and 5.1:1 (dark); Edo 6.9:1 and 6.6:1. In the default palette the two blues barely differ. In Edo the line shifts tone where it enters the screengrab, which reads as crossing onto another surface. The target box and the end dot follow the same rule.
  • R3, chosen (15 Oct 2026). R2 with the paper casing softened at its outer edge, the restrained halo cartographers prefer to a hard one. The casing stays fully opaque and only its edge is blurred (σ 0.7px), so about 91% paper survives across the first pixel beside the line: at worst ≈3.3:1 at 1× (3.7:1 at 2×), about 18% below a hard casing. Because of that, a midtone page is one below ≈3.65:1, not 3:1. A halo that was partly transparent as well would fall to 3.0:1 at 85% opacity, the 3:1 limit, and 2.4:1 at 75%. The softness must come from the blur, never from opacity.
  • The selection spotlight (F) now dims towards the paper as well: a white page fades towards white, a dark page towards dark. That is the cartographers' direction for an area of disinterest, and it no longer greys a white page in dark mode.
