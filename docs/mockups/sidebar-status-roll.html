Commentary · README

Recommendation

Ship the fixed order, which is already decided. Do not ship the count. Keep this page as a parked option for the day two long idle jobs overlap.

  • Why not the roll now. The pair overlaps for about 30 seconds, then Screens comes back by itself (readiness doc §8, decision 3). A roll buys 30 seconds of seeing Screens, at the price of motion on a line the researcher glances at all day.
  • Why not borrow it. You asked for prior art. I found no shipping product that rolls between concurrent jobs in one status line (table below). The verified convention is one ambient line plus a separate place that lists everything. A roll would be invention.
  • Why not the count. "+1" announces a second job that nothing can open. In this app a glyph or count earns a door or it is noise (SubtitleGlyphAction), and "absence is information". Of the four it makes the loudest promise for the least.
  • If you build it anyway: crossfade, not roll; 4 s dwell; only among same-tier idle activities; never over a row that has a glyph; Reduce Motion falls back to the fixed order. Bench setting "Reduce Motion falls back to" shows both fallbacks.

What the repo already says

  • Hard rule, design-desktop-project-status.md §5: never put two conditions on one line; the detail pane is where the non-winners get room. One sanctioned exception composes (.completedPartial). A roll is two conditions on one line spread over time, so it amends the rule rather than fitting it.
  • The idea is already named. The comment on SidebarSubtitleText.tooltip calls "a cycling departure-board line that shows each condition in turn" the intended direction, and says not to grow the tooltip. I found no design doc behind it, so it is a direction, not a spec. The only shipped departure-board motion is the project-icon split-flap (design-project-icons.md, a decelerating flip), which is the nearest house vocabulary for a roll.
  • Shimmer is .running only today (shimmerSubtitle), behind "Show animation while analysing" and Reduce Motion. Readiness decision 5 extends it to the Signals line. The bench shimmers Signals, and the 2700 ms period against a 3 s dwell means each entrance shows about one sweep.
  • Width. About 22 characters at 240 pt. The running ladder and the German Signals phrase (27) both truncate; the full form is the tooltip. A count must sit outside the truncating text, as drawn.

Honest costs

  • Attention. A glance surface all day. The rule I used (roll only among idle activities, never over a glyph row) keeps the roll to the rare overlaps, but the overlap that triggers it is the very pair you named, right after every analysis.
  • Battery. One timer and two short layer animations per dwell, far less than the shimmer's continuous sweep. Not measured.
  • VoiceOver and live regions. A roll must be silent: the row's accessibility value lists both jobs once, statically, and an announcement fires only when the set of jobs changes. The bench log shows exactly that: flips are silent, starts and finishes speak.
  • Two moving things. Shimmer already means "the AI is thinking". A roll moves the whole line every few seconds while the shimmer sweeps within it. On the pair it is the shimmering Signals line that rolls in and out. Judge this in the bench by leaving the shimmer checkbox on.
  • One job hidden at any instant. Fixed order hides Screens for about 30 s with certainty. The roll shows each for most of the time but a glance can still catch the other one. For 30 s the fixed order is more honest; for hours the roll would be better.
  • Counts tick mid-dwell. "3 of 40" becomes "4 of 40" while visible. The bench updates text in place with no roll, as a native version must.
  • It stops being a single-winner chain. Today ProjectSubtitle.resolve returns one SubtitleVariant and every glyph, door and tooltip switch is exhaustive over it.

What a native version would take (not built)

  • Model. A resolveAll beside resolve returning the ordered same-tier idle activities (and exactly one entry when anything has a glyph). Pure, table-testable: which line at which tick, settle on the survivor, jump to a newcomer. This part is cheap and testable.
  • View. The subtitle is an AppKit cell. A fixed-height clipping view holding two text layers; the roll is a CATransition push or a CABasicAnimation on position, the crossfade a layer opacity animation. SwiftUI's own text content transitions (a default crossfade; a digit roll for numbers) are the framework's version, from my memory of the API and unverified here.
  • The sharp edge. ProjectSidebarOutline.update() reloads the outline about once a second during a run, which would tear down an in-flight animation and restart the dwell every time. The roll clock has to live in the controller, keyed by project, not in the cell. The same hazard is already handled for inline rename (editingNodeID) and the icon reveal, so there is precedent, but it is the real cost.
  • Gates. Reduce Motion via NSWorkspace.shared.accessibilityDisplayShouldReduceMotion (already read for the shimmer); pause when the window is not key or the row is off screen; an NSTrackingArea for hover; a static accessibilityValue; NSAccessibility announcements on set changes only.
  • Size. Roughly one to two days with tests, plus a policy edit to §5. No new locale strings for the roll itself (the Signals key is its own work). Not an estimate I would defend to the hour.
