It is the shipped Counter button, as on the Quotes and Screens lenses, not a blue text link, so the only link on a card stays Show all / Show fewer. Scope decided: the hidden quotes of the section or theme the page shows, not the project, read live from the database, not from the snapshot. A quote hidden before the snapshot was built is listed even though it never reached a card (the third line here), so it can be unhidden from here but returns to its card only after the next rebuild. That is the case the build must handle: rebuild or refetch, or accept a quote that reappears with no card to show it. An unhide from here is also a quote moving while the researcher watches, so the Quotes lens’s fly-down and .bn-handover question applies. Measured in WebKit: the counter ends on the heading’s keyline edge, 56px in from the pane edge, and the open list (439px wide, 112px tall) is right-aligned to that same edge, opens at the bottom of the line and overlays the first card by 60px. In the 780px pane it starts 285px inside the pane; in the 520px pane it starts 25px inside it and runs 31px past the cards’ left edge, within the pane. Putting a padded button on the line costs rhythm: the banner moves down 2.3px and the first section starts 4.8px lower than the real page, which has no button there. With nothing hidden the counter draws nothing and the page is exactly the real one (the table below); to keep that, the banner’s own 12px bottom margin is moved from the paragraph to the line. What this does not show

Commentary, not product.

  • The written headline and the evidence assertion can still paraphrase or cite a hidden quote until the card is regenerated. Here the assertion cites Q4, which is shown in every frame, so the problem is not drawn.
  • The all-hidden edge case (every quote on a card hidden) is deliberately not drawn; it is parked.
  • Cards are not re-ranked or re-scored when a quote is hidden: the signal, strength and ordering of cards change only at the next run or codebook apply.
  • The refill uses the plain rank order from the brief. The shipped selectQuotes also holds one of the four slots for a dissenting voice; whether it re-runs on the remaining quotes is a build question this mockup does not decide.
  • The inspector heatmaps and the “Based on N tags” banner come from frozen matrices and are not redrawn here; whether the banner’s count follows a hide is still open. Heatmap cell counts, though, will follow the hide (owner decision), so a cell that says 6 leads to a card that says “Show all 6”. Not drawn.
  • The hero chip’s label and flag, the card’s strength (0.62 here) and the card ranking stay as calculated; hiding a quote changes none of them until the next run or codebook apply.
  • Contribution cell levels are relative within the card, so hiding one participant’s quote can change the shade of another participant’s cell. In frame F, p2 is darker in B than in A once Q6 (p1) and Q7 (p2) are gone, because the cells are ranked against each other. The client’s card-folding re-runs over the remaining quotes, so the card the researcher sees is the folding of what is left, not a filtered copy of the original.
  • Frame E is parked, not v1. Its counter is the Quotes lens’s own, so the shipped counter does not grey a quote in place: it opens a list you click to unhide.
