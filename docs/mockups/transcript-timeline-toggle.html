The control: a split button, the Mac's pull-down-with-default

The button half does the common thing (chain ↔ timeline); the pulldown holds the choices. That's the shape of a Mac split button (a SwiftUI Menu with a primaryAction, Mail's Reply / Reply All). It's drawn as two segments of the house segmented control, .dimension-toggle, so it needs no new control vocabulary.

Button: icon + "Timeline". Pressed, it takes .dimension-btn.active as shipped (hover tint, body ink, emphasis weight; accent text fails AA at 12 px) and only the icon turns accent. Below a 720 px row (a container query, so it follows the sidebars, not the window) the label goes and the icon stays.

Pulldown: the Export menu's classes and check gutter. The first group is the same toggle, said in words (both strings shipped). The second picks the detail; picking one also turns the timeline on, since that's plainly what was meant.

Does a marker track scroll? A range does, a playhead doesn't

Reading is a range, not a moment, so scroll moves a reading window: the time from the paragraph under the header to the last one on screen, interpolated within each, once per frame. It uses the minimap's viewport tokens, and dragging it scrolls the page as the minimap's does. The current section's flag stays raised, by the chain's rule (the last waypoint above the header's bottom + 8 px).

The playhead stays a player thing: the thin hover cursor is the shipped one; a heavier accent mark with a head shows the player's time, only while media plays. Press Play: PlayerContext already centres the playing paragraph, so the window follows the player without new code.

A click scrolls, never navigates

  • Flag: the chain's own handleIndexClick.
  • Speaker lane: resolveSegmentForSeconds, the resolver #t- deep links already use, with the anchor flash.
  • Sentiment bar: straight to its quote; no popover, since the quote is on the page.
  • The URL takes #t-<s> by replaceState. The readout under the frame shows what would be written.

Opening it keeps your place

The header grows; scrollTop is compensated so the paragraph under the header is still under it, and --bn-journey-header-height is re-measured (the mode and the detail join that effect's deps). Changing the detail keeps your place the same way.

The right end of the sticky row. Top two rows: a wide row, light then dark. Third row: a narrow row (under 720 px), icon only. Last: the pulldown open.

Words

The details are named by how much they show, not by a lane count: "3 lanes" is our geometry, not the researcher's question. The hint says what each adds.

On the Mac

The View menu mirrors it: Show / Hide Session Timeline with the SF Symbol, then a Timeline Detail ▸ submenu with Compact, Standard and Full, enabled on a transcript and greyed elsewhere. No keyboard shortcut proposed (shortcuts are earned).

Keyboard

Two Tab stops (button, chevron). The chevron opens the menu with Return, Space or ↓; arrows move, Return picks, Esc closes and gives focus back to the chevron. The menu items are menuitemradio, as the Export menu's burn-subtitles row is a menuitemcheckbox.

3 · The glyph: an SF Symbol and its Lucide pair

Lucide is drawn here at 40, 20 and 14 px (14 is what the control uses). SF Symbols are named, not drawn: a browser engine never draws them (house rule, design-icon-system.md).

Recommended: timeline.selection ↔ gallery-horizontal

SF Symbols has no Gantt chart. I rendered every timeline-ish symbol on this Mac (macOS 27, from the system's own catalogue). timeline.selection is Apple's own timeline glyph: a filmstrip frame held between two trim handles, ▶▮◀. It's what Photos and QuickTime trim with, our welcome screen already calls the tapestry a "video-editing style timeline", and the fat state is a range held on a timeline. First shipped 2020, so macOS 11, well inside the 15.0 floor.

Lucide's gallery-horizontal is its silhouette twin: a frame between two uprights. On its own Lucide calls it a gallery, but beside the word "Timeline", and as the twin of the Mac's glyph, it reads right.

Rejected

  • chart-gantt ↔ calendar.day.timeline.left, iteration 1's pair. Rendered, the SF glyph is a calendar day view (an event with a now-line), and the two don't look alike.
  • align-start-vertical ↔ align.horizontal.left: an exact shape match (an axis, two bars), but both mean "align left" on their own platforms, and on the Mac it would sit beside real alignment commands.

Check the SF half in the SF Symbols app: search "timeline".

4 · How tall each detail is

Each frame is the sticky slot alone at the live frame's width, same reading window, same current section. The bars measure each against the window height chosen above; the tick is one third.

The choice is the researcher's now

Standard (sections, speakers, sentiment) is the default: it's the most that stays under a third of a laptop window, and it answers "where am I, who's talking, how did it go".

Iteration 1 shed lanes automatically on a short window. With the pulldown, the page no longer second-guesses: if the menu ticks Full, Full is what's drawn. Shedding behind a ticked item would make the menu lie. So the edge case is named, not handled: Full on a 720 px window takes about two fifths, and Standard on a 560 px one about the same. Compact is one click away.

The tapestry's 14 px under the timecodes is room for macOS's overlay scrollbar; with no zoom in the slot there's never a scrollbar, so it could go. A cheap saving, not taken here.
