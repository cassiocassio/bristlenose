Every value is extracted from source: the card DOM from QuoteCard.tsx, its CSS verbatim from six theme files, the focus transform from focus-mode.css, the φ arithmetic from WelcomeHomeView.swift + GoldenSplit, the window sizes from BristlenoseApp.swift, and the timings from WelcomeBaton.swift. 1 · The live cell — true pixel size, real card CSS, real transform

The φ arithmetic, in full: the spiral is framed width: w, height: w / 1.618 with .padding(20), so w = window − sidebar − 40. GoldenSplit(.horizontal) hands the major cell width: (w − 8) × 0.618 and the spiral's full height w × 0.618 — square to within the gutter's share. 3 · What the harness is for

  • The card is not what the ideas page drew. The real blockquote.quote-card has a left border only and is rounded on the right only (border-radius: 0 6px 6px 0); the layout is a hanging indent — .quote-row is a baseline-aligned flex with the timecode in a left gutter and .quote-body carrying text, speaker and badges. Timecodes render with square brackets, [11:30]. The earlier draft drew a full box with a meta row underneath, which was an impression of a quote card rather than the product's one.
  • The dissolve touches two properties, not four. background: transparent and border-left-color mixed to 40% — there is no full border to dissolve. The recede set is exactly .badges, .timecode, .context at 0.14, plus headings at 0.4. .speaker is not in it (axis 1 keeps "whose voice" lit) and neither is .star-btn — there is no Focus rule for the star anywhere in the system, which the illustration inherits for free by having none either.
  • Timing is two clocks, and mixing them is the easy mistake. Beats stretch by WelcomeTempo — speed 0.6 → stretch 1.667, and a tool webview carries pace 1.3, so ×2.167 — plus a 3s rest on the opening frame and 3s on the finished frame. The 320ms dissolve does not stretch: micro-transitions stay at authored speed by stated policy, because slowing a fade reads as lag rather than calm. So the loop is slow and the transition is not.
  • At the minimum window the slot is GONE, and that is the headline finding. Derived from source, sidebar out at 220: a 700pt window gives a 272pt cell, and the furniture — tag 14 + title 20 + a two-line summary 32 + CTA 15 + nav row 26 + drop card 78 + six 8pt gaps 48 + 32 cell padding — is ~265pt. That leaves about 7pt. Not "cramped": arithmetically nothing. At the 1000pt shipping default the cell is 457pt and the slot gets ~192pt, which is why the pool’s caps cluster at 160–196 — they are tuned to the default window and are already at its limit. At 1600pt it is ~563pt and everything is easy. So the design target is the default window, and the minimum window is a degradation case every illustration already handles by self-scaling — which is exactly why BookShelfView’s fan reaches 0.29 there. Collapse the sidebar and 700pt becomes a 408pt cell, which is survivable; it is the sidebar-out minimum that is brutal.
  • The first-draft copy was the longest line in the pool, and copy length is a spacing decision here. Measured against the eight shipped slots: Miro 28 chars, clips 38, codebooks 55, starHide 65, autocode 66, agent 69, tag 84, ingest 96 — and the 21 Sep draft of this line, 110. At the 700pt window’s ~36 characters per line that is four lines against the pool’s typical two, spending ~26pt of a budget that is already at zero. The default here is now the cut at 83 chars, written inverted-pyramid with an em-dash hinge so WelcomeClauseFit can drop the trailing clause and leave a grammatical 33-char core — the rewrite discipline the 20 Aug pass adopted across all 45 slots. Switch Copy to compare the three.
  • The spacing question has a measurable answer, and it is not flattering at the minimum window. The furniture is a near-constant ~210–230pt while the cell is square and scales linearly, so the slot's share collapses as the window narrows. Switch Slot between cap 176 and fill to see the two mechanisms: every shipped illustration takes a cap and self-scales under it, while fill is the full-bleed proposal that measures its box and stops when the next card will not fit. Only the second answers "how many quotes" honestly at every size.
