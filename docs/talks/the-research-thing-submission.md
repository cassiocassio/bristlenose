## Short description for your talk

> I'm a practising user researcher. The two days after fieldwork are the ones
> I dread: a folder of recordings, a deadline, and an AI that will write my
> findings for me if I let it. So I spent a year building an open-source tool
> that makes the AI show its working instead.
>
> This is the story of what went wrong — a calibration run on data too thin to
> mean anything, a transcriber that hears "thank you" in silence, a model that
> hedged when it should have committed — and the rules that survived: which
> quote earns a slot, when to name a feeling, and what the AI must never
> decide.
>
> Nothing to sell. Every rule works with the AI you already have.

## What attendees will learn from your talk

> - A workflow for the two days after fieldwork where the AI proposes and you
>   decide: one place per quote, every claim with its numbers showing, every
>   call yours to overrule.
> - Quote-cleaning rules you can paste into any prompt: filler out and the cut
>   marked, self-corrections kept, every added word in brackets — participants
>   sound like themselves on a good day, not like someone else.
> - When to let the AI name a feeling and when to make it say "mixed": by the
>   amount of evidence, not the balance — and why one quote in four is held for
>   the person who disagreed.
> - Three failures and what they taught: calibrate on data shaped like your
>   real studies, expect a transcriber to invent politeness in silence, and
>   decide up front what the AI must never do — for me, synthesis, ranking,
>   recommendations and statistical claims.
