Commentary

Hover a session's speaker lane. After the open delay a frame appears: the screen 25% into the participant turn under the pointer, from the real recording. It steps from turn to turn, it does not scrub. Click still jumps to that moment in the transcript. The first session is a 38-minute usability session with 45 participant turns and frequent page changes; the second is an 18-minute Teams call with 7 long turns, where the first minutes are the call and then the share begins.

The defaults, and why

  • Open after 700 ms, reopen at once within 1 s. A sweep across the lane to reach a sentiment mark should not flash a picture; a researcher who is reading the frames should not wait for each one. The warm rule is the macOS tooltip's.
  • Swap after 100 ms of rest. At the fitted zoom a 20-second turn is a sliver a few pixels wide, and swapping on every crossing would churn images on a fast sweep.
  • Close grace 300 ms. Crossing from one session's lane to the next keeps the preview armed instead of blinking.
  • Moderator turns hold the last participant frame. The picture does not change when the moderator speaks (the colour lab measured this across 25 recordings), so holding is truthful and stops the preview strobing through every exchange.
  • 160 px wide, below the tracks (the owner's call, 13 Oct 2026). Large enough to tell a call from a web page from a terminal, too small to read text. Below, the section flags stay in view, so the frame and the section name it falls in read together; it covers the sentiment marks instead, which are not being used while the pointer is on the lane. Above, it also spilled over the row above when that row was open.
  • Outline on the source turn. Says which turn the frame came from, which matters while the moderator turns hold.

One frame per scene (the default here, 13 Oct 2026)

A scene is a stretch between two colour changes in the strip, whoever is speaking: the preview steps when the screen changed, not when the speaker did. Measured on these two sessions before choosing the defaults:

  • Scenes at ΔE 8, not the notches' 14. At 14 a scene is not colour-stable inside (p90 spread 8.5 in the usability session), so one frame does not stand for it. At 8 the spread is under 0.5. That gives 31 scenes against 45 participant turns in the usability session, and 18 against 7 in the Teams call.
  • 2 s in, capped at half the scene. A frame at the change itself is mid-transition in 13–22% of scenes (more than ΔE 5 from the scene's own colour); 1–2 s in, 0–6%; 5 s in it gets worse again, because some scenes last 6 s and the next transition arrives.
  • Flickers under 2 s get no frame; the preview holds the scene before.
  • The limit is colour. Two web pages with the same palette are one scene to this measure. Per turn catches those only when the speaker changes too. Switch the mode and the threshold to compare.

What building it implies

  • The preview cannot live inside the slice. The slice clips its overflow (it scrolls sideways), and the space above the tracks is 26 px. It needs a portal or a fixed layer, as here.
  • Frames come from serve, after the report, like the strip. One seek per participant turn: a 45-turn session is about 45 small JPEGs, a few hundred KB, under .bristlenose/intermediate/, served by an authenticated route like the session thumbnail. They are participants' screens, so the HTML export leaves them out; the strip already summarises.
  • No new strings. The caption is a timecode. Mouse-only and aria-hidden, the same call the review made for section flags.
  • Not pixel-identical. The slice here is the 6 Oct design template, close to SessionTapestry.tsx but not its tuned geometry. The preview itself uses the shipped tokens; its two-layer shadow is invented.
  • Notches are off, as parked today.

To judge

  • Is one frame per turn enough to find the first screen share, or a switch to a terminal, in the usability session?
  • Does 700 ms feel deliberate or sluggish? Does holding over moderator turns read as truthful or as stuck?
  • Width: does 120 still tell environments apart; does 240 start to compete with the timeline?
  • Pointer-following versus pinned to the frame's moment: which makes the turn it belongs to clearer?
